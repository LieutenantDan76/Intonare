# How Daniele writes quiz blurbs

Read this file before writing or editing any quiz pack content. These
rules were built from every triage pass across theory_fundamentals,
bass, beatles, guitar_technique, eighties, guitar_gods, and seventies.
They survived six rounds of hand-editing. The beatles and
guitar_technique packs are Daniele's own writing and the only voice
reference. Other packs are the AI's approximation of that voice.

Target word counts by pack type:
- People packs (beatles): ~42 words
- Decade packs (eighties): ~33 words
- Instrument packs (guitar_technique): ~29 words
- Theory packs (theory_fundamentals): ~27 words, one clause


## The #1 rule: ONE SENTENCE

One sentence is the overwhelming default. Daniele joined 20 of 24
edited blurbs from two sentences back into one.

- Join with a COMMA or SEMICOLON, not a period.
  "X happened, and Y followed" or "X happened; Y followed."
- Two sentences are fine ONLY when the second adds a genuinely separate
  fact that doesn't flow from the first.
- When in doubt, join. The hit rate went up dramatically once the blurbs
  stopped being chopped into two.


## Semicolons vs commas vs periods

- SEMICOLON: two clauses of equal weight about the same subject.
  "The wah gave it a tone close to guitar; Morley eventually made a
  signature pedal based on his setup."
- COMMA + AND: cause and effect, or a continuation. "He started playing
  higher up because his amp was too small, and kept doing it because it
  became the band's sound."
- PERIOD: genuinely separate facts. Use sparingly.
- Never bolt a tangent on after a semicolon. The semicolon FINISHES the
  thought.


## What stays, what goes

KEEP:
- Names, dates, numbers, places (these are the substance)
- Etymology and origin ("Italian for," "named after," "comes from")
- The specific detail that makes it a did-you-know (butter knife, boat
  epoxy, bicycle brake cable)
- Player-facing context (where a shape sits on the neck, what a tool
  does for you)

CUT:
- The second thread, unless it flows as one sentence
- Subjective quality opinions: "warm," "heavy," "muddy." But
  RESULT descriptors are fine: "punchy" (what happens when bass
  and kick land together), "swaying" (what the rhythm does). The
  line is: does the word describe what happens, or how good it is?
- Reader-mind-reading: "most listeners don't notice," "nobody realizes"
- Explaining why something matters: "which is why," "which is how,"
  "that's what makes"
- Stock phrases: lives here, nobody since, built on, the whole trick is,
  still the standard, ever since, on purpose, in his life, at all,
  before or since, famously, iconic, legendary, celebrated
- Teacher voice / instruction: "bass players count the subdivision
  rather than assuming a speed from what the notes look like"
- Historical tangents that aren't the point: "Leo Fender designed it
  more than a decade after selling the company" -- if the history IS
  the blurb, keep it; if it's a tail detail, cut it
- Tail trivia about model numbers: "It sat on the EB-0 and EB-3 for
  most of the sixties" -- product names as an appendix get dropped


## Player-facing framing

Describe what the tool or technique does FOR the player, not the
mechanism. "Adds presence to normally quiet dynamics and keeps loud
notes under control" not "turns loud notes down so quieter ones come
up." Say what it was used FOR specifically: "for their hip hop
basslines" not "just as producers started wanting one."


## Tail trimming

Daniele consistently cuts the last clause when it:
- Restates what was already said
- Is opinion or instruction
- Is a loose fact that doesn't flow ("He died the year after" -- cut)
- Explains the mechanism nobody asked about ("because you're stopping
  it at exactly half its length" -- cut)


## Softening absolutes

- "the bass either doubles it" -> "the bass usually doubles it"
- "so close to guitar that listeners couldn't tell" -> "close to guitar"
- "every beat" is fine, but add "tend to" or "usually" when describing
  what players do (they don't all do it the same way)


## Adding specificity

Daniele adds detail when the AI was vague:
- "in the right spot" -> "fifth fret"
- "the octave" -> "with the octave laying on the next string up from
  there"
- "initially sold badly" -> "initially sold badly" + what happened next


## Stems (questions)

- Keep stems SHORT and CLEAN. No clues.
- The stem asks, the blurb explains. If a clause in the stem is
  telling the story ("until they stopped blowing up," "before dying at
  thirty-two," "with only two working fingers"), it belongs in the
  blurb. Cut it from the stem.
- Test: does the stem still identify the answer without the clause?
  If yes, the clause is blurb material.
- A hook that identifies the answer stays ("recorded while two couples
  inside the band were splitting up" is what makes Rumours answerable).
  A detail that explains the answer goes.
- "Which bass did McCartney play?" not "Which bass did McCartney choose
  partly because it looked right left-handed?"
- "Which pedal effect did Cliff Burton use?" not "Which pedal did Cliff
  Burton run his bass through?"
- Remove model names and pronouns that give it away.
- "sits on" -> "is on"; "carried the melody" -> "preferred playing"
- Questions must be worth asking. If the question exists just to say
  the answer ("Which song puts a bass figure under a four-minute
  intro?"), it needs a rewrite. The question should make the reader
  think, not just set up a namedrop.


## Options

- Never put the answer's detail in the stem where only one option fits.
- Add artist names to album/song options when multiple artists appear
  ("Thriller -- Michael Jackson").


## Word choice

- "song" not "record" (unless about the physical object)
- "their" not "his own" when the answer is a person
- "blew up" not "blew" (for amps)
- "let" not "lets" for past tense
- Contractions preferred
- No exclamation marks. ONE per pack allowed when genuinely funny.


## The omega check

Read every row whole: stem + options + blurb.
1. Does the blurb echo the answer?
2. Do the options still answer the stem?
3. Does the blurb hand over another question's answer?
4. Does the stem give it away with a pronoun or detail?
5. Is the last clause opinion, instruction, or restated? Cut it.
6. Is the question worth asking, or does it just exist to say the
   answer?


## Write what a player would say

This is the principle under everything. When in doubt, say it the way
a real player would say it to another player.

- "Made their name on" is AI cadence. A player would say "found in a
  lot of metal such as Iron Maiden."
- "Under most funk and blues bass lines" is a sweeping generalization.
  A player would connect it to a specific shape they know: "falling in
  line with the minor pentatonic shape."
- "Nearly impossible to pick out as a separate part" is too heavy an
  opinion. A player would say "hard to hear separately."
- When a blurb is generic but the question is specific, make the blurb
  specific too. A question about A7 needs a blurb about A7, not about
  chord symbols in general.
- Always defer to what a real player would hear, say, or know. If the
  sentence wouldn't come out of a musician's mouth, rewrite it.


## The AI tell that gave it away

The pattern Daniele could feel was uniform rhythm. Any fixed two-beat
shape repeated across blurbs reads as machinery. A second sentence must
answer something the first raised, not just add the next true fact.
Facts can stack, but opinions cannot be shoehorned in.

Phrases that are never acceptable:
- "lives here," "nobody since," "built on," "the whole trick is"
- "ever since," "in his life," "obvious to everybody in the room"
- "still the standard by which..."
- "most listeners never notice"

A semicolon continues the same thought; it does not park a tangent.
Anchor the blurb to the song or player the stem names.
A teacher's voice, not a critic's.


## Italian translation rules

- Music jargon stays English in Italian: pocket, walking, ghost note,
  one-drop, tumbao, shuffle, box (pentatonic shape), slap, strum,
  pickup, setup, signature, fretless, feedback, backbeat, riff.
  Using Italian equivalents ("scatola" for box, "schiaffeggiare" for
  slap, "strimpellare" for strum) sounds wrong to Italian players.
- "Andare in diretta" means going on broadcast, not going direct to
  a mixer. Use "entrare diretti nel mixer."
- Read every Italian blurb against the English before install. The
  gate catches structure but not wrong register.
- Before installing, read every Italian blurb against the English for
  literal or wrong-register phrasing (19 fixes were needed on bass
  alone: "in modo paludoso", "andare in diretta", "scatola" for box,
  "strimpellare", "schiaffeggiare" for slap).
- When replacing a pack's questions array, match the quiz block by
  `<id>: {` PLUS its `group:'...', name:'...'` line. A bare
  `\bbass\s*:\s*\{` matches the tuner's instrument table first and
  overwrites a different pack's array. Verify every ready pack's row
  count after install.
