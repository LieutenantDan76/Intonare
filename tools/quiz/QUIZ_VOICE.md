# QUIZ_VOICE.md — read this before writing a single stem

This file exists because QUIZ_SPEC.md does not work on its own. The spec is
fourteen hundred lines of rules ABOUT the voice; the voice itself lives inside
Intonare.html, which nobody opens before writing. So every session the style gets
reconstructed from a description, and a description of a voice is not a voice.
Each rebuild lands close and drifts a little, and the drift is always the same
direction: toward the spec sheet.

**The rule: read the twelve questions below, in full, before writing anything.
Then write. Then read them again and compare.** The spec explains why these work.
It is not a substitute for having just read them.

---

## The three tests, applied to the stem before anything else

**1. Could one person ask another this across a table?**

    yes   Which string does Keith Richards take off the guitar in open G?
    no    What does an active bass need that a passive one does not?

**2. Is there a way in for somebody who does not know?** The answer should be
reachable by reasoning from the stem, not only by already holding the fact.

    yes   Why did Marshall start building amplifiers as two stacked cabinets?
    no    How many watts did the Ampeg SVT put out when it arrived in 1969?

**3. Would the player repeat the answer to somebody else afterward?** This is
the fun test and it is the one that keeps getting lost, because a question can
pass every mechanical check and still be about nothing.

    yes   Which Chicago bluesman played with a hundred-foot cable so he could
          leave the club?
    no    What does an 808 kick do on a phone speaker?

## What the twelve have in common, counted rather than felt

- **Eleven of twelve are about a person doing something.** Not a technique, not
  a spec, not a signal path. Even the amplifier question is a story about Pete
  Townshend asking for something stupid.
- **The answer is a name or a thing you would say out loud.** Slash. Frippertronics.
  The low sixth. Not a number, not a definition.
- **The stem carries one concrete image.** A battered Stratocaster called Number
  One. A hundred-foot cable. A school uniform since 1974. The image is what gives
  a player a way in.
- **The blurb goes somewhere the question did not**, and often lands on one human
  detail: Nobody has found it since. He still plays it to this day!
- **Nothing is asked twice.** Twelve questions, twelve different shapes of ask.

## The failure mode this file exists to stop

Writing a technically correct question about a true fact that nobody would ever
ask anybody. It passes the pack audit, passes the voice audit, reads fine in
isolation, and makes the pack feel like a manual. The symptoms, all of which
happened in the Bass medium tier:

- a wattage
- what a component does
- what happens on a phone speaker
- a hyper-specific cue standing in for a question ("comes in after the acoustic
  harmonic on which track")
- a fun fact included because it was surprising to ME, not because anybody would
  enjoy being asked it

# The twelve, from the shipped Guitar Gods pack

These are the approved artifact, extracted from Intonare.html. Not rewritten,
not paraphrased.

**What has Angus Young worn on stage since 1974?**

> A superhero cape · A school uniform · A convict's stripes · A boiler suit

Angus tried a gorilla suit, a Zorro mask and a Superman cape before his sister Margaret suggested the school uniform, lending him her son Sam's blazer from Ashfield Boys High for a Sydney gig in April 1974.

**How did Brian May get his most famous guitar?**

> It was a gift from Jimi Hendrix · He built it with his father · He bought it secondhand in London · He had it made by Fender

Brian built it with his dad at the age of 16, making the neck from an 18th-century fireplace mantel and the body from an old oak table, filling the wormholes with matchsticks. He still plays it to this day!

**Which Beatles song did Prince play his celebrated solo on at the 2004 Rock and Roll Hall of Fame?**

> While My Guitar Gently Weeps · Something · Here Comes the Sun · Taxman

Prince stood in the dark for most of the Harrison tribute in 2004, alongside Tom Petty, Jeff Lynne and George's son Dhani, then took a two-minute solo and threw his guitar into the air at the end. Nobody has found it since.

**Which rock guitarist played the solo on Michael Jackson's Beat It?**

> Jeff Beck · Slash · Eddie Van Halen · Steve Lukather

Van Halen did the solo for nothing as a favor to Quincy Jones, rearranged the section underneath it before he played a note, and reckoned the whole thing took about twenty minutes of his life.

**Which string does Keith Richards take off the guitar when he plays in open G?**

> The third · The fifth · The low sixth · The high first

Richards tunes to open G and pulls the low string off entirely, leaving five, so the bottom end stops fighting the bass player. Start Me Up, Honky Tonk Women and Brown Sugar all come out of that tuning.

**Which guitarist played a battered Stratocaster called Number One, put together from mismatched parts?**

> Rory Gallagher · Buddy Guy · Robert Cray · Stevie Ray Vaughan

Number One had a 1963 body and a 1962 neck as far as anyone can tell, with the letters SRV stuck on the pickguard, and he played it hard enough to need it refretted over and over.

**Why did Marshall start building amplifiers as two stacked cabinets?**

> Venues limited how wide a cabinet could be · Two cabinets were cheaper to ship · The amp overheated inside one box · A single eight-speaker cabinet was too heavy to move

Pete Townshend asked Jim Marshall for eight twelve-inch speakers in one box and waved away the warning about his road crew. Two weeks later he came back and asked for it cut in half, which is where the stack comes from.

**What was the name of the tape-loop system Robert Fripp built with Brian Eno?**

> Discreet Music · Oblique Strategies · Soundscapes · Frippertronics

Two Revox reel-to-reel machines side by side, with the tape running from one to the other, let Fripp play over what he had played thirty seconds earlier. Eno's album Discreet Music came out of the same rig, and Fripp toured it alone through the seventies.

**Which singer built her songs around dozens of invented tunings?**

> Joni Mitchell · Carole King · Carly Simon · Judy Collins

Joni Mitchell had polio as a child and lost strength in her left hand, so she retuned the guitar until the chords she wanted fell under the fingers she could still use. She ended up with more than fifty tunings.

**Which guitarist did flamenco players credit with opening the form up?**

> Paco de Lucía · Sabicas · Vicente Amigo · Tomatito

Paco de Lucía brought a cajón back from Peru and put it into flamenco, where it replaced a great deal of hand clapping, and he played jazz with John McLaughlin while purists complained he had ruined everything.

**Which Chicago bluesman played with a hundred-foot cable so he could leave the club?**

> Buddy Guy · Otis Rush · Magic Sam · Junior Wells

Buddy Guy would walk out into the street mid-solo while the band kept going, and the stage tricks a young Eric Clapton and Jimi Hendrix later made famous were things they had watched him do first at the Chess sessions and the South Side clubs.

**Which Australian did Chet Atkins name a Certified Guitar Player?**

> Tommy Emmanuel · Slim Dusty · Phil Emmanuel · Keith Urban

Chet Atkins handed out that title to a handful of players in his lifetime, and Tommy Emmanuel had been playing professionally since the age of six, touring Australia in a family band with his brother.

# Blurb voice: the thirteen approved rewrites

Daniele's five from Guitar Gods and his eight from the Bass pack are in
QUIZ_SPEC 2b with the before-and-after on each. The three moves:

1. Cut the closing sentence that carries a verdict rather than a fact.
2. Fold a small related fact into the sentence as a participle instead of
   tacking it on as a five-word sentence.
3. Break a distinct second idea into its own sentence rather than hanging it off
   an "and". A fact that modifies the subject already there folds in; a fact that
   introduces a new subject gets its own sentence.

# The omega read

The last step, and the one that works. Every question in the finished pack, in
order, stem and options and blurb together on the screen, read as a person would
read them. No script, no filter, no grep.

On the Bass pack this ran after the pack audit, the voice audit, the leak, style,
duplicate and trim checks had all come back clean, and after three rounds of
hand-fixes. **It found eleven more.** The three that matter, because no tool can
see them:

- **Three questions whose options had stopped answering their stems.** I had
  rewritten "The fretless solo does what in its second half?" into "How did the
  engineers make it?" and left the options reading "repeats the first half
  backwards". Every audit passed. The question was nonsense.
- **Two stems still inverted**, missed by the regex because they do not end in
  "what?": "...is said to be playing where?"
- **A blurb handing over a different question's answer** in words the
  cross-check does not match on.

The rule that falls out of it: **when a stem changes, its options and its blurb
are stale.** Re-read the whole question, not the line you edited.

Ask of each one, in this order:

1. Would a person ask this out loud, in these words?
2. Do the options answer the question as it is now written?
3. Does the blurb finish the idea, or does it stop early or run on?
4. Does the last sentence follow from the one before it?
5. Is anything here given away by, or given away to, another question?
